Instructions

How to use Gunshy Fix

Results come from structure and letting the dog progress. Not from skipping ahead to “see if they’re over it.”

1 Set the right environment

Play the lessons in a quiet, familiar place — crate, kennel, or a room the dog already likes. The dog should be relaxed before you hit play. Do not run this during stressful training, corrections, or chaos.

2 Always start with Lesson 1

Lesson 1 is music only. That is the point. Do not skip it because it “seems quiet.” Move to Lesson 2 only when the dog is calm and happy on Lesson 1.

3 Pair it with something good

Meals, a bone, calm petting, rest. Not pressure. Not a correction. The sound should predict something the dog already likes.

4 Be consistent

Daily is best. Four or five days a week at a minimum. Play the whole track you are on. Repeat it until the dog is clearly fine — then go forward.

5 If they change, back up

Watch for shutting down, glued-to-your-leg, leaving the room, panting, freezing, or stopping work. That is not “almost ready.” Go back a lesson and stay there until they are themselves again.

6 Then go to the field

When the dog is comfortable with the program at a high volume — close to a real shotgun — take the speaker outside. Walks, retrieves, hanging out. Then live gunfire: start at least 200 yards away, gun pointed away from the dog, dog already doing something it loves (food, bumper, bird).

If they hesitate, add distance immediately. If they have decided the gun itself is scary, let them see it without firing while a lesson plays, then carry it, then shoot again at a distance.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I skip Lesson 1?

Lesson 1 is music only. That is the point. Do not skip it because it “seems quiet.” Move to Lesson 2 only when the dog is calm and happy on Lesson 1.

How often should I play the lessons?

Daily is best. Four or five days a week at a minimum. Play the whole track you are on. Repeat it until the dog is clearly fine — then go forward.

What if my dog’s demeanor changes?

Watch for shutting down, glued-to-your-leg, leaving the room, panting, freezing, or stopping work. That is not “almost ready.” Go back a lesson and stay there until they are themselves again.

What mistakes should I avoid?

Common mistakes: skipping tracks, turning the volume way up by hand, playing it during already-anxious moments, and treating fear like something you can correct out of a dog.
